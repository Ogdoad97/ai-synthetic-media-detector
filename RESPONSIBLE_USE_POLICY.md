# Responsible Use Policy

This policy explains the intended use of the AI Synthetic Media Authenticity Detector and how the maintained project responds to safety concerns. It is guidance for project development and operation; it does not amend or replace the repository's MIT licence.

## Intended beneficial uses
Examples include media-literacy education, journalism and fact-checking support, academic research, defensive investigations, provenance analysis, and helping people decide when further verification is needed.

## Uses requiring particular care
Do not treat a detector score or heatmap alone as sufficient grounds to accuse, punish, dismiss, deny services to, or publicly identify a person. High-impact decisions require independent corroboration, human review, and an opportunity to challenge the conclusion.

Operators should avoid uploading private or sensitive media without an appropriate legal basis and consent where required. Do not expose uploaded files, results, or task identifiers to unauthorised parties.

## Misuse and limitations
The project recognises that media analysis tools can be misused to falsely label authentic media as synthetic, lend unwarranted authority to uncertain outputs, target or discredit people, expose sensitive media or metadata, or evade safeguards through a downstream fork.

The upstream project will assess concrete misuse reports and consider proportionate responses such as clearer warnings, safer defaults, documentation changes, mitigations, or release holds. Actions should be evidence-based, documented, and reviewed for unintended impact on legitimate users.

## Reporting a concern
Until a dedicated private security contact is published, do not post exploit details, credentials, or private media publicly. For sensitive vulnerabilities, contact the repository owner through an appropriate private GitHub channel first. The project currently has no guaranteed response-time SLA.

For non-security concerns, open an issue with a concise description, affected version or commit, reproducible steps where safe, likely impact, and a suggested mitigation if known. Do not upload real people's sensitive media or personal data into public issues.

## Maintainer response
Maintainers should triage reports, preserve confidentiality where feasible, assess severity and affected versions, and document the chosen response. A fix should include regression tests when practical. Public disclosure should be coordinated with affected parties when appropriate and should avoid publishing exploitable details before mitigation is available.

## Limits
No policy can guarantee that every user will behave responsibly, and the MIT licence permits broad reuse subject to its terms. This policy governs the maintained upstream project's practices; it cannot force all downstream forks to adopt it.
